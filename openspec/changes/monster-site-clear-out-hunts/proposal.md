## Why

Design §6 approves bound clearing as the second hunt semantic and §5 approves authored camps, nests, and boss sites as encounters with their own lifecycle. Both halves exist today — `monster-site-placement` owns three authored sites with owned individuals and a durable one-shot/recoverable state, and `monster-quest-objectives` made bound clearing strict (only the record's bound individuals ever count, and a recovered site's fresh individuals can never substitute an old binding) — but nothing connects them: a bound stage today can only be bound by the scene materializer's own spawned encounter, so the three authored sites hold individuals that no commission ever refers to. Publishing a clear-out over an authored site is therefore new behaviour in three places at once: the objective needs a lawful way to name the site, acceptance needs a guarantee that the site can currently supply bindable targets (and a named refusal when it cannot), and the board must not offer work over a one-shot site that has already been cleared. This is the change that turns the placement wave's authored encounters into commissions.

## What Changes

- Add the site clear-out selector to the objective vocabulary: `QuestObjective.site_key`, legal only together with `requires_bound_targets=True`, mutually exclusive with `monster_tier` and with the regional species-hunt selector, validated against `MONSTER_SITE_REGISTRY`, with a quantity at or below the site's authored capacity. The bound set remains the site's own living individuals — the objective names a source, never a spawn — and the generative compile boundary cannot author the selector (a hand-written-only field), while the stored payload round-trips it with an absent-key default.
- Add the acceptance-time guarantee and the stage-zero binding for a site clear-out. Before the record is written, the deterministic core asks the site owner for the site's living individuals and refuses (named reason: world absent, unknown site, site cleared, or fewer living individuals than the quantity) when the site cannot supply them. When it can, acceptance binds exactly those individuals as the record's stage-zero objective targets through the existing binding writer, inside the same all-or-nothing transaction as the record write — with no instance pin, because the site is a permanent wilderness location, not a spawned scene. The acceptance path never creates, moves, recovers, or populates a site: a site's population and its recovery stay the site owner's clock-settled lifecycle, and a never-populated or cleared site simply refuses.
- Make the board offer set availability-sensitive for site clear-outs only: `list_guild_offers` omits a bound clear-out whose site cannot currently supply its targets, so a cleared one-shot site's commission stops being offered and a recoverable site's commission reappears when its authored in-game condition has matured and it has repopulated. Rank eligibility, ordering, and the summary rendering are untouched, and the availability read is the same one acceptance uses, so the board can never advertise what acceptance would refuse. A regional species hunt is unaffected: its guarantee provisions on demand and is not lifecycle-bound.
- Publish the three bound clear-outs, each naming its authored site and carrying its own rank, rating rationale, background flavor, and rank-banded reward:

  | definition key | site | lifecycle | bound individuals | quantity | rank | copper / merit |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `ridge_burrow_nest_clear_out` | ridge_burrow_nest | one-shot, western_hills_valleys | burrow_maker, nest_guard | 2 | E | 250 / 60 |
  | `cliff_echo_camp_clear_out` | cliff_echo_camp | recoverable 86400 ticks, western_hills_valleys | cliff_stepper, pass_warden | 2 | D | 1400 / 140 |
  | `tide_mouth_boss_site_clear_out` | tide_mouth_boss_site | one-shot, southeast_coast | bay_warden | 1 | D | 1800 / 160 |

  Clearing a one-shot site is a durable authored victory: its commission is offered until it is cleared and never again, because the site never repopulates on its own. Clearing the recoverable camp is repeatable, and the fresh individuals a recovery creates can only be bound by a commission issued after their creation — the landed strict-binding rule, now exercised by shipped content.
- Record the new boundary events in the observability logging design's catalog (site targets bound, site targets unavailable with its refusal vocabulary) and emit them through the `world.observability` facade, with site, region, species, variant, required, available, and reason context keys and no player-facing prose in logs.
- Update the player-visible documentation in the same change, because the board's offer set is now availability-sensitive: `docs/game/commands.md`'s `guild list` row and `docs/game/command-reference.md`'s `guild list` section state that the board lists work that can currently be completed, so a cleared site's commission is absent, and `tests/test_command_docs.py` stays green.
- `quest-progress-tracking` needs no change: the landed strict-binding requirement already states that only the record's bound individuals count and that recovered newcomers can never satisfy an old binding — this change's content exercises it rather than extending it.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- None. (The selector family extends `quest-blueprint`, the guarantee and binding extend `quest-lifecycle`, the offer set extends `guild-quest-board`, and the site owner's read extends `monster-site-placement`.)

### Modified Capabilities

- `quest-blueprint`: registration gains the site clear-out selector family (site key, bound flag, quantity within the site's capacity) and a new requirement states that a site clear-out binds the site's own individuals, never spawns, and cannot be authored through the generative compile boundary.
- `quest-lifecycle`: acceptance gains the site clear-out guarantee (site state as the sole supply, named refusals, no creation or recovery) and the stage-zero binding of exactly those individuals inside the same transaction, with no instance pin.
- `guild-quest-board`: board listing gains the availability rule for site clear-outs (with rank eligibility, ordering, and summary rendering unchanged) and the acceptance path's refusal now covers a site clear-out's named refusal with no partial state.
- `monster-site-placement`: a new requirement states that a site's living individuals are the quest layer's binding source and that neither acceptance nor the board may create, populate, or recover a site for a quest.
