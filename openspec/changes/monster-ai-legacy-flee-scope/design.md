# Design

## Context

The shipped dispatch in `world/rules/monster_behaviour.py` resolves an effective decision mode and branches once: utility goes to `_utility_dispatch`/`_utility_action` (`_utility_action` enumerates, estimates, scores and samples candidates), everything else goes to `_legacy_action`, whose first act after finding living enemies is `_should_flee` (the sole production call site of `_should_flee`, `monster_behaviour.py:1006`). No module under `world/rules/monster_ai/` references flee or disengage at all: the utility candidate families are damage, healing, control and support skills, and the authored score terms are `damage`, `kill`, `heal`, `control`, `focus`, `cost`, `waste`, `preference`.

The main spec `openspec/specs/monster-flee-policy/spec.md` nonetheless states its threshold-resolution, inclusive-boundary and priority requirements against `monster_behaviour_policy()` with no mode scope, so three of its seven requirements read as promises the utility path does not keep. The retained contract is authoritative elsewhere: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-monster-ai-design.md` section 1 ("Existing `legacy` behavior is an explicitly retained decision mode and rollback control ... Its target selection, skill order, flee priority, and dice sequence remain unchanged"), its "Deliberately unchanged contracts" section ("Monster flee thresholds, `FLEE_SKILL_KEY` and flee priority remain outside scoring"), `openspec/specs/monster-action-policy/spec.md` ("Legacy mode SHALL retain the following existing contract. ... Declared flee thresholds SHALL retain inclusive priority."), `openspec/specs/monster-ai-rollout/spec.md`'s rollback scenario ("legacy target, skill, flee priority and dice behavior"), and `docs/development/monster-ai.md`'s activation section, which records that the enabled variants no longer follow legacy flee priority.

Because `openspec/specs/` is the current-contract index and `tools.spec_traceability` reads only it, the wrong text is not merely cosmetic: it is the contract any future change is measured against.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Make the three mode-ambiguous flee requirements explicitly legacy-scoped, keeping their requirement identities and their existing scenarios.
- Give the utility mode's no-voluntary-flee behavior its own requirement, so "flee stays outside the utility score" is a contract rather than an inference from source.
- Establish both sides at one authored threshold with a deterministic synthetic behavior test.
- Keep the change to a spec delta, one test module, one shard registration and one changelog line.

**Non-Goals:**

- No production behavior change. `world/rules/monster_behaviour.py`, `world/rules/monster_ai/` and `world/rules/rulebook/monster_behaviour.yaml` are not edited.
- No production activation change: the shipped `variant_decision_mode` set stays exactly as the archived `monster-ai-staged-activation` left it.
- No change to `monster-action-policy`, whose flee clause is already legacy-scoped, and no change to the four remaining flee-policy requirements. The request-shape and orchestration requirements describe a flee request once one has been selected; the archetype-declaration requirement is load-time validation that gives `null` a disabling meaning without requiring a non-null threshold to trigger flee; and the determinism requirement is mode-neutral once state and RNG inputs are fixed. None of them asserts that a utility decision must flee.
- No new flee tuning, no new score term, no player-facing command documentation, no migration, no compatibility layer.

## Decisions

**Amend the specification, not the engine.** The shipped utility path is the approved design, so adding a flee branch to the utility dispatcher would change settled production behavior, contradict design section 1 and the "Deliberately unchanged contracts" list, and require its own evidence campaign. The delta therefore only narrows existing text and adds the missing utility clause. Alternative rejected: leaving the spec mode-ambiguous — it would keep the conflict live and misattribute the utility behavior to a defect.

**Scope by `MODIFIED`, and put the utility contract in one `ADDED` requirement.** A `MODIFIED` requirement keeps its identity, so the existing behaviour tests stay attached to the requirement they actually verify; the utility contract is new behavior text and gets a new identity with its own evidence. Alternative rejected: rewriting the boundary requirement to cover both modes — that would attach two different roles to one identity and blur which mode an annotation claims.

**Attach the utility clause to exactly the requirements the utility path contradicts.** The threshold-resolution requirement (a utility monster must not derive flee from the resolved threshold), the inclusive-boundary requirement (the predicate is legacy-only), and the priority requirement (the branch order constrains the legacy body only). The request-shape requirement describes a flee request once selected, the orchestration requirement consumes whatever the policy returned, the determinism requirement is mode-neutral, and the archetype-declaration requirement is load-time validation — none of them asserts that a utility decision must flee, so none is touched.

**Qualify the prohibition over the utility decision, and keep the typed invalid-state fallback intact.** The shipped dispatch has two deliberate entry paths into the legacy body for a utility-mode monster, and both converge on `_invalid_state_action` (`monster_behaviour.py:1162-1177`), which calls `_legacy_action` under the configured fallback: an invalid resolution found before mode dispatch routes through `_degraded_action` (`monster_behaviour.py:983-984`), and an invalid runtime state raised by the utility decision itself is caught by `_utility_dispatch` (`monster_behaviour.py:1211-1223`). That fallback contract is owned by the `monster-ai-utility-selection` requirement "Invalid state fallback is narrow and observable". An unqualified "a utility monster never flees" would therefore contradict shipped behavior in exactly the case the invalid-state contract exists for. The delta therefore states the prohibition over the utility decision itself, adds the same qualification to all three scoped requirements, and gives the exemption its own scenario with a synthetic case that injects the typed error at the boundary and establishes that the fallback still returns the legacy inclusive flee request. Alternative rejected: forbidding flee even under the fallback — that would change settled behavior and would need its own evidence.

**Do not promise a scored request unconditionally.** `_utility_action` legitimately returns `None` when capability is blocked or when enumeration and scoring yield no selection (`monster_behaviour.py:1312-1347`, `1371-1372`), and the utility-selection capability already requires that empty boundary. The added requirement therefore promises a scored selection only when one exists and says explicitly that the no-action boundary is its own, never the legacy flee request, with a synthetic control that owns no skill and returns no action at and below the threshold.

**Establish the contract with a paired-mode test at one authored threshold.** File-local synthetic data is authored once: one archetype with a non-null `flee_hp_fraction` authored `legacy`, one registered variant bound to it, one threat tier, one real `Monster` row per case, and one MP-costing damage row. Only the effective decision mode differs between the two cases (`modes={}` for legacy, `modes={VARIANT: "utility"}` for utility), and each case asserts its resolved mode and a null invalid reason before calling the policy, so the assertions isolate the mode and not the fixture. Threshold `0.5` with maximum HP `100` makes the inclusive boundary (`50/100`) exact in binary floats, so the boundary case needs no tolerance and no re-derivation of the engine's own comparison. The utility side additionally reads the opt-in decision trace, so the test shows a *scored* selection (`candidates >= 1`, a `selected` plan whose `skill_key` is the returned request's key) rather than merely "not flee". Nothing here needs a session scope, a seed or the combat dice: one legal candidate and the canonical inspection identity make the decision reproducible on replay, which the below-boundary test asserts.

**Claim "never an enumerated candidate" on the enumeration itself, not on the trace.** `DecisionTrace.candidates` is a count and `plans` holds scored candidates, so a faulty implementation that enumerated the flee key and then dropped it during estimation would still satisfy trace-only assertions. The candidate test therefore wraps `world.rules.monster_ai.enumerate_candidates` with a recorder that forwards every argument unchanged and preserves the returned `CandidateSet`, and asserts the flee key is absent from every skill key that set reports — candidates, rejections, exclusions and unusable skills — as well as from the reported plans and the returned request, sweeping the HP range. The innate overlay stays in place there, so the monster demonstrably owns the canonical flee key while nothing it decides with ever enumerates it.

**Fixture reuse.** The new module imports `SyntheticTuningMixin`, `_scope`, `_suppress_innate_skills`, `STRIKE`, `SPECIES`, `VARIANT`, `TIER` and `ARCHETYPE` from `world.rules.tests.test_monster_ai_selection` (the precedent for cross-module fixture reuse is that module importing probes from `test_monster_ai_snapshots`) and installs its own archetype table through `open_tuning(archetypes=..., modes=..., intelligence=...)`, because the selection module's own archetype factory hardcodes a null `flee_hp_fraction`. The module uses the shared kit factories (`world.tests.synthetic_data.make_monster_species`/`make_monster_variant`/`make_monster_tier`) transitively through that scope helper, and real rows through `construct_species_individual`. A separate module is justified because the paired-mode assertion needs the tuning-patching, registry and DB-row harness the other monster-AI behaviour modules use, while `test_monster_flee_decision.py` is a fake-entity module without rows or a tuning loader; extending it would mix two fixture styles in one file. Alternative rejected: adding the case to `test_monster_ai_activation.py` — that module is the shipped-activation data-contract carrier and names the shipped bestiary, which this synthetic mechanism test must not do.

**Shard ownership.** The new label `world.rules.tests.test_monster_ai_flee_scope` goes into shard index 2 (`rules-b`), which already owns every `test_monster_ai_*` and `test_monster_*behaviour*`/flee module. No shard balancing rule exists (`tests/test_evennia_test_optimization_contract.py` enforces exactly-one ownership, resolvability and stale-label removal only).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The scoped text could be read as license to remove the legacy flee branch later] → the added requirement ends by pinning the legacy inclusive priority, and the three scoped requirements keep their existing threshold/priority scenarios verbatim.
- [A new module must be registered or the ownership contract fails on every branch] → the manifest edit lands in the same change, verified with `tests.test_evennia_test_optimization_contract` and `tools.contract_gate`.
- [A paired-mode test that patches two tunings in one method could leave a stale patch] → `SyntheticTuningMixin.open_tuning` registers its `patch.multiple` stop on `addCleanup`, so both installs unwind with the test; the two monsters are constructed inside their own case.
- [Synthetic fixtures could drift from the shipped archetype shape] → the file-local archetype table carries all six authored fields (`target_strategy`, `skill_choice`, `prefer_area_when_multiple_enemies`, `flee_hp_fraction`, `decision_mode`, `utility_weights`) through the production loader, so a drifted loader fails the load instead of the assertion.
- [The utility prohibition could be read as forbidding the typed invalid-state fallback and invite a production change] → the exemption is stated in all four affected requirements and established by its own synthetic case at the boundary.
- [The added requirement could be read as promising an action when the utility decision legitimately returns none] → the requirement separates the scored selection from the no-action boundary, and the empty-candidate control establishes the boundary.

## Gate phasing

The added requirement's identity resolves against `openspec/specs/` only after this change's delta is synced there, and `tools.spec_traceability.py` rejects an annotation whose identity is absent from that index (`unknown-requirement-id`). The repository already documents this phase split (`docs/development/spec-test-traceability.md`, "Static verification": the archive check is authoritative after sync, and a pre-sync pass does not guarantee a post-sync one). Implementation therefore proves the post-sync state on a scratch tree whose source roots are hard-linked from the repository and whose `monster-flee-policy` spec has the delta applied, and runs the pre-sync gate subsets that are unaffected (`observability`, `test-data`, `manifests`, `contracts`). The later sync/archive workflow owns the green aggregate `tools.contract_gate` and the in-repo traceability check. No allowlist, waiver or weakened workflow is introduced, and the two scorings happen in opposite phases by design. Alternative rejected: syncing the delta during apply — that is the archive workflow's authorized step, not this change's.

## Migration Plan

None. This change adds no persistent state, no schema, no configuration default and no compatibility path; it is a specification correction plus evidence. At archive time the three `MODIFIED` requirements overwrite their counterparts in `openspec/specs/monster-flee-policy/spec.md` and the `ADDED` requirement is appended; nothing else in the main spec index moves.

## Coverage and annotation review

`tools.spec_traceability list` against the equivalent delta-applied tree yields the unchanged IDs for the three modified requirements and `monster-flee-policy::utility-mode-never-voluntarily-flees` for the added one, with no normalization collision (`2190 requirements, 0 errors` on the merged tree, with exactly the added requirement uncovered while no test exists). No identifier is retired and no annotation is re-mounted: the current annotations are

- `monster-flee-policy::flee-tuning-follows-existing-tier-defaults-and-instance-overrides` → `world/rules/tests/test_monster_flee_decision.py::MonsterFleePolicyTests.test_all_tier_defaults_and_override_have_reproducible_decisions`
- `monster-flee-policy::the-policy-checks-current-to-maximum-true-hp-at-an-inclusive-boundary` → `world/rules/tests/test_monster_flee_decision.py::MonsterFleePolicyTests.test_non_positive_maximum_does_not_divide_or_choose_flee`
- `monster-flee-policy::flee-selection-has-priority-over-attack-selection-without-consuming-decision-dice` → `world/rules/tests/test_monster_flee_decision.py::MonsterFleePolicyTests.test_flee_precedes_skill_selection_and_consumes_no_policy_roll`

All three run without an installed synthetic tuning, so their fake monsters take the legacy body and they assert exactly the scoped legacy behavior; the review confirms they remain valid claims under the narrowed text and need no weakening. The new module adds the boundary case for the legacy side in the paired-mode test, so the re-scoped predicate also carries mode-aware evidence, and the added requirement's five scenarios are claimed only by the new module's methods: the paired boundary assertion, the below-boundary replay, the empty-candidate control, the enumeration-sweep candidate test, and the typed invalid-state fallback case.
